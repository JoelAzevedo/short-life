# Little Moments — story bible

> “We are born so tiny, get older, and then time passes so fast that we need to enjoy
> the little moments that pass by — like the sweet memories we have with our children.”

## Genre

A **narrative adventure / interactive memoir** in isometric low-poly dioramas, in the
tradition of *Florence*, *What Remains of Edith Finch* and *Before Your Eyes*. You live one
whole life in eight chapters. There is no score, no game over. The only “mechanic” that
matters is **attention**: each chapter is full of small glowing *moments*; when you engage
with one, time slows and you can **hold Space to keep it** — a real snapshot of your
playthrough goes into the album. Moments you ignore, or that you are too slow to keep,
pass. At the end the album is all you have.

## Core systems (and how they carry the theme)

| System | Emotional job |
|---|---|
| **Keep a moment** (hold to photograph) | Being present is an act. The window to keep a moment shrinks in the chapters where life goes fastest. |
| **Life clock** (top right) | From Chapter III on, the day keeps moving while you wander. It **pauses during moments** — when you’re present, time stops. |
| **Work** (blue blinking hotspots, Ch V) | Answering emails/calls skips the clock forward and costs moments. There is no reward. The final album counts the emails. |
| **Camera zoom** | Tiny in Ch I (the world is enormous), widest in Ch VI (life seen from far away), intimate again at the end. |
| **Colour grade** | Pastel dawn → saturated summer → golden → dusk → washed-out fast-forward → near-monochrome winter, where colour returns *only around your grandchild*. |
| **Music** | One lullaby threads the whole life: a music box in your nursery, your mother humming, your grandfather whistling, minor-key piano at a funeral, your own voice to your child, your grandchild humming it in the snow, the whole orchestra at the end. Generated live with Web Audio; layers swell with intensity. |

## Cast

- **You** — the player chooses at the start whether you’ll grow up to be *a mother* or *a father*.
- **Mom & Dad** — your parents. Mom sings the lullaby.
- **Grandpa & Grandma** — Grandpa whistles the lullaby, plants the tree with you, gives you his watch.
- **Biscuit** — the family dog. A puppy with you as a baby; old and slow by your teens.
- **Theo** — your best friend next door.
- **Sam** — the kid at the ice-cream cart at 13; the person you marry at 26. (They/them.)
- **Your child** — you choose a daughter or a son and name them.
- **Pip** — your grandchild.

## Places that return

- **The nursery** — your room (Ch I), your child’s room (Ch V), their teenage room, the empty room (Ch VI).
- **The yard** — the house, the street, the cherry tree, and **the family tree** you plant at 5 with Grandpa (Ch II) — knee-high in Ch III, holding your wedding in Ch IV, carrying a swing in Ch V, bare and enormous in Ch VII.
- **The kitchen** — the prologue, pancakes (Ch V), two cups of tea (Ch VII).

## Chapters

0. **Prologue — The Album.** A winter night, the kitchen, an almost-empty album. *“Let’s go back.”*
1. **I · Tiny (0–1).** Crawling through the nursery: the mobile, a sunbeam, Biscuit, blocks, Mom’s lullaby, first steps into Dad’s arms. The first spring in the garden: petals, grass, a butterfly, your first word, asleep on Grandpa’s lap. No clock.
2. **II · Wonder (5–7).** Summer. Planting the tree with Grandpa. Sandcastles with Theo, a lemonade stand, a scraped knee, fetch with Biscuit. Night: fireflies in a jar, stars with Dad, marshmallows, “read it again”. The clock appears, but gently.
3. **III · Running (12–15).** Bikes, the town, Sam at the ice-cream cart, skipping stones with Dad, walking old Biscuit slowly. **Grandpa on the porch asks you to sit with him** — you can say “later”. Then the rain; his chair is empty. Paper lanterns. *If you said later, the game remembers.*
4. **IV · Together (22–28).** The lantern festival: meeting Sam again, the waltz, the rowboat. The wedding under the family tree.
5. **V · Little Ones (30–40).** The heart. Your child is born in your old nursery. You sing your mother’s song. First steps (mirrored: *“Three steps. You cried.”*), the swing on the tree, teaching to ride a bike, puddles, the drawing, bedtime questions — *“Will you always be here?”*. Work keeps blinking.
6. **VI · So Fast (40–55).** One yard, seasons spinning faster and faster, your child growing visibly every few seconds, moments flickering up and vanishing. The music won’t stop accelerating. Then the car pulls away, and everything stops.
7. **VII · Winter (75+).** A quiet house, two cups of tea. Then your child comes home with Pip, and colour comes with them. A snowman, the tree, Grandpa’s watch, and Pip humming the lullaby — three generations later.
8. **VIII · The Little Moments.** A path of light lined with the photographs you kept. Each step makes you younger. At the end you are tiny again. The album, the count of moments, and one request: *close the game and go find your little ones.*

## Writing rules

- Second person for narration (“You…”). Short sentences. Concrete, sensory, small.
- Never explain the theme; let the mirrors do it (first steps, the lullaby, “read it again”).
- Characters speak plainly and warmly. No villains. Loss is gentle and shown, not described.
